The story

London is a good city. But good cities can drift when nobody is clearly in charge of the basics.

People are working hard, paying more, waiting longer, and wondering why everyday city problems feel so difficult to fix.

That frustration is not left wing or right wing. It is the feeling of sitting in traffic beside another construction delay. It is the small business owner wondering whether downtown will come back. It is the family watching housing costs outrun common sense. It is the resident who wants compassion for people in crisis and also wants parks, sidewalks, and storefronts to feel safe.

John Feher’s campaign is built around one clear promise: make London’s local government more practical, more focused, and more accountable to the people who live here.

The operating principle

Less performance. More performance metrics.

Londoners should not have to decode City Hall. They should be able to see what is being worked on, who is responsible, what it costs, and whether it is improving.

Priorities

Five priorities. No theatre.

The campaign should be judged by whether these priorities are clear enough to repeat at the door and concrete enough to govern from.

01

Move housing faster

Shorten approval delays, support responsible density, and make the process easier to understand for builders, neighbours, and families.

02

Restore public confidence downtown

Focus on cleanliness, safety, storefront activity, lighting, events, and a visible plan that gives residents and businesses a reason to return.

03

Fix the basics people feel daily

Roads, construction coordination, transit reliability, snow response, permitting, bylaw response, and customer service should be managed like core services.

04

Coordinate crisis response

Homelessness, addiction, mental health, shelter, outreach, policing, and housing supports need one accountable operating table, not scattered excuses.

05

Respect the taxpayer

Budgets must connect spending to visible outcomes. If residents are paying more, they deserve to know exactly what improved.

The standard

Every major promise should come with a public scorecard.

Plain-language reporting on housing approvals, downtown confidence, construction disruption, transit reliability, infrastructure backlog, service response times, and budget performance.

London, street by street

A serious campaign listens differently in every neighbourhood.

London is not one issue. Downtown, Old East Village, Byron, Lambeth, White Oaks, Argyle, Masonville, Hyde Park, Wortley, and Westmount all experience the city differently.

For families

Affordability, parks, traffic, schools, recreation, safety, and a city that feels easier to live in.

For small business

Permits, storefront confidence, parking, construction planning, foot traffic, and cleaner commercial corridors.

For seniors

Transit access, snow clearing, safe crossings, community services, and respectful neighbourhood growth.

For young Londoners

Housing options, public spaces, arts, employment, transit, and a city worth staying in.

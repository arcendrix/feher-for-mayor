London, Ontario municipal election

Practical leadership for a city under pressure.

John Feher is running for mayor to bring focus, accountability, and common-sense execution back to City Hall.

Londoners are dealing with the same problems every day: housing that is too expensive, roads that feel poorly coordinated, public spaces that do not always feel safe, and a downtown that needs confidence again. The job of a mayor is not to perform concern. The job is to organize people, set priorities, and get measurable work done.

Why John is running

London needs a mayor focused on the work residents actually feel.

People are not asking City Hall for theatre. They are asking for a city that feels safer, cleaner, easier to move through, easier to build in, and easier to trust.

This campaign is built around practical municipal leadership: set the priorities, publish the progress, respect the taxpayer, and stop hiding poor execution behind complicated language.

The measure of success is simple: can residents see and feel improvement in their neighbourhoods?

The plan

A serious platform starts with the basics.

These are not abstract campaign themes. They are the operating priorities a mayor can use to organize council, staff, partners, and public reporting.

01

Make housing approvals faster and clearer.

Reduce unnecessary delay, publish approval timelines, identify bottlenecks, and make responsible growth easier to navigate for builders and residents.

02

Restore confidence in public spaces.

Support a cleaner, safer, more active downtown and neighbourhood main streets with visible standards, better coordination, and regular reporting.

03

Fix the daily service issues people notice.

Roadwork, traffic disruption, snow response, transit reliability, bylaw service, and permitting should be managed like core services, not excuses.

04

Coordinate crisis response with accountability.

Homelessness, addiction, mental health, outreach, shelter, housing supports, and enforcement need clearer coordination and public-facing outcomes.

05

Respect the taxpayer.

Every major budget decision should connect spending to visible service improvement. If taxes rise, residents deserve to know what actually got better.

Accountability

Campaign promises should not disappear after election day.

John’s approach is to publish plain-language progress updates on the issues residents care about most: housing approval timelines, construction disruption, downtown conditions, service response times, transit reliability, and budget performance.

London, neighbourhood by neighbourhood

Different parts of London feel different pressures.

A mayoral campaign has to listen across the whole city — not just downtown, not just one ward, and not just the loudest voices online.

Families

Affordability, parks, traffic, recreation, safety, and basic services that work.

Small business

Construction planning, storefront confidence, permits, parking, and cleaner commercial corridors.

Seniors

Safe crossings, transit access, snow clearing, community services, and respectful growth.

Young Londoners

Housing options, public spaces, arts, work, transit, and a reason to build a future here.

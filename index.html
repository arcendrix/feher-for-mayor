The case for change

London is not broken. But too many basic systems feel harder than they should.

Residents are not asking for political theatre. They are asking for a city that feels organized, safe, affordable, and responsive.

That means housing approvals that move with urgency. Roads and construction planned with discipline. A downtown that businesses and families believe in again. Compassionate support for people in crisis, paired with clear standards for public spaces. And a budget that respects every household already feeling squeezed.

John Feher’s campaign is built on a simple idea: London can be run better.

The practical plan

Five priorities for a city under pressure.

The goal is not to promise everything. The goal is to focus City Hall on the issues that affect daily life most.

01

Build homes faster

Speed up approvals, remove unnecessary delays, support smart density, and focus on housing regular working families can actually afford.

02

Fix the daily commute

Treat traffic as a quality-of-life issue. Coordinate construction better, improve key corridors, and make transit more reliable.

03

Restore downtown confidence

Support a cleaner, safer, more active downtown where small businesses, residents, students, and visitors want to spend time.

04

Coordinate crisis response

Bring homelessness, addiction, mental health, outreach, enforcement, and housing supports into one accountable operating model.

05

Respect the taxpayer

Focus spending on visible service improvements, publish clear progress updates, and make City Hall easier to understand.

Neighbourhood focus

A city-wide plan has to work street by street.

London is not one problem. Byron, Argyle, White Oaks, Wortley, Old East Village, Downtown, Masonville, Lambeth, and Hyde Park all experience the city differently.

The campaign will listen neighbourhood by neighbourhood, publish what residents are saying, and connect each concern to a clear action plan.

Community first

The best campaign asset is proof that John listens.

A serious campaign should not just talk at people. It should collect what residents are experiencing and show how those concerns become policy.

Small business owners

What would make London easier to operate in, hire in, invest in, and believe in?

Families

What is making daily life harder: rent, traffic, taxes, safety, childcare, or access to services?

Frontline workers

Where are systems failing on the ground, and what practical changes would reduce pressure?

Students & young adults

What would make the next generation choose to stay, work, build, and buy a home here?
